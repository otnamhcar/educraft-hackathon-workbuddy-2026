[FIELD] PROJECT DESCRIPTION
----------------------------------------------------------

PROJECT OVERVIEW
Challenge C, The Creator Agent. EduCraft turns one approved curriculum source
into a complete primary-school teaching pack: lesson plan, pre-test, post-test
with answer key and scoring, slide outline, student article, infographic brief,
and a playable HTML quiz. Target users are IPAS teachers of grades III-VI
(Fase B and C) in Indonesian primary schools, and the curriculum coordinators
who must audit their materials.

What makes it different: the pack is produced by four cooperating agents, then
checked sentence by sentence against a written rulebook of 12 rules, and it
cannot be published until a human approves it. Run of record:
EDU-ORCH-20261026-001, topic "Energi Alternatif", anchored to curriculum outcome
C4 (2.1.4) with foundation B5 (1.1.5).

Where it came from: the idea, the curriculum references and the substance of the
rules came from don Jimat Akbar, a practising IPAS primary-school teacher. He set
which claims a teacher will never put in front of a class; our work was to
formalise that judgement into rules a machine can check and to build the pipeline
that enforces them. The rulebook, not the code, is the artefact a school could
adopt - a curriculum team can edit it without touching the pipeline.


REAL-WORLD INSIGHT
The pain point is not that teachers cannot write. It is that they cannot afford
the review loop. An Indonesian primary teacher carries 20-30 contact hours a
week and must still produce a per-topic teaching pack, where every sentence has
to survive four questions: is this number in my source, is this too frightening
for a ten-year-old, does it still match the curriculum, and would this create a
health claim I cannot defend.

Generic chatbots make this worse. They produce fluent text with no citation
discipline and no record, so the teacher ends up fact-checking a machine, which
costs more time than writing from scratch.

EduCraft moves the checking inside the pipeline. The reviewer agent is a gate
with a written rulebook, a verdict, and a citation of the exact rule violated.
The teacher's remaining job is a judgement call on already-checked material.


SOLUTION DESIGN
Business layer: Orchestrator holds state and enforces limits; Planner decomposes
the source into per-format outlines mapped to curriculum outcomes; Writer drafts
using only tagged source facts; Reviewer checks 12 rules and returns APPROVE or
REJECT with quoted text. A teacher or SME then opens the approval gate. The
Reviewer can veto but cannot rewrite - an agent that can grade and fix its own
work will eventually grade itself generously.

Technical layer: four WorkBuddy skills, each a SKILL.md contract plus read-only
assets (source material, curriculum outcomes, rulebook R001-R011, and a
real-world claim register where every entry carries an official URL and an
access date). Output contract: {format, status, iterations, final_content,
findings[], sources[]} plus an append-only audit log.

Prompt design is the enforcement mechanism, not decoration. Every fact must
carry a [src:#] tag or the writer emits [UNVERIFIED] instead of guessing. Every
rejection must quote the offending sentence and cite the rule. The revision loop
is capped at three, after which the run halts and escalates to a human. Source
documents are treated as data, never as instructions, which is our
prompt-injection defence. Publishing is a separate gated step, so the agent
cannot ship on its own authority.


BUSINESS VALUE
- Five formats plus an interactive quiz from a single source, in one run.
- Estimated 6-8 hours of drafting and checking per topic reduced to roughly
  30-45 minutes of reviewing already-checked material. (Estimate from our own
  artefact volume; to be validated in field testing - we are not claiming a
  measured result.)
- 12 rules checked per document, each with a pass/fail and a citation, versus
  0-2 informal checks today.
- One real error caught before publication: on 29 October the reviewer rejected
  our own output for broken source grounding. We fixed the source layer,
  re-tagged every draft, and re-reviewed to APPROVE. Both reports ship with the
  submission.
- Full audit trail: inputs, decisions, findings, revision history v1 to v2.

Scales beyond the hackathon because the engine is domain-parameterised: swap the
source file, the curriculum file and the rulebook, and the same four agents
produce material for another subject or grade band. The rulebook is plain
Markdown a curriculum team can edit without touching code.

[FIELD] ACCOUNTABILITY SHEET  (one page — see 03_Accountability_Sheet.md)
----------------------------------------------------------
Summarise in four blocks. Full text is in the file.

CAN ACCESS (read-only): the approved source material (the only permitted well of
facts), curriculum outcomes, rulebook R001-R011, the real-world claim register,
the run configuration, its own prior drafts and findings, and an anonymised
student score table. No internet access during a run, no external accounts, no
student personal data.

EXECUTES AUTONOMOUSLY: decomposing the source into per-format outlines; drafting
all five formats with citation tags; self-review against all 12 rules; re-drafting
after a rejection up to three times; writing the audit log; refusing to proceed
when a source is missing, contradictory or out of scope.

REQUIRES HUMAN APPROVAL: publishing or distributing any pack (every time);
overriding a REJECT; accepting a claim marked [UNVERIFIED]; changing the source
material or the rulebook; using output outside the intended grade band. Current
state: PENDING - nothing has been published.

WILL NEVER: publish without explicit approval; invent a fact not in the source;
present an unverified real-world claim as fact; write an unsupported health
claim; promote a harmful practice; use fear as a teaching device; follow
instructions embedded in a source document; loop past three attempts without
escalating; expose student identity; hide a failure.

[FIELD] ARCHITECTURE  (see 05_Architecture.md + architecture_diagram.png)
----------------------------------------------------------
Four agents in a loop, three trust boundaries, one human gate.

Teacher brief and approved source enter the Orchestrator, which writes the run
configuration and routes work. Planner produces per-format outlines mapped to
curriculum outcomes. Writer drafts with citation tags. Reviewer checks the 11
rules and returns a verdict with the rule cited and the sentence quoted. A
rejection goes back to the Writer, capped at three iterations, after which the
run halts and escalates. An approval passes to the human gate; only then is
anything published.

Trust boundaries: (1) read-only boundary around the knowledge assets - the agent
cannot edit the source or the rulebook to make its own output pass; (2) human
authority boundary at the approval gate - reviewer approval is a precondition,
not a permission; (3) publish authority boundary - there is no path from Writer
to output that bypasses the gate. A fourth implicit boundary treats document
content as data, not instructions.

Key trade-offs, stated as costs: rules live in the prompt rather than in a
fine-tune, which keeps them auditable by a curriculum team but makes adherence
sensitive to rulebook length. A single reviewer rather than an adversarial pair,
which is cheaper and faster but has no second opinion - mitigated by requiring
every verdict to quote text and cite a rule, so a human can audit the audit.
Strict grounding over coverage, which makes the agent look less clever than a
general chatbot on thin sources, but targets the failure mode that destroys
trust. A hard cap of three iterations, which may abandon salvageable drafts, but
avoids a loop that talks itself into approving.

[FIELD] PRODUCT SHARING  (see 06_Product_Sharing.md)
----------------------------------------------------------
Where WorkBuddy added value: treating a skill as a contract rather than a prompt.
Each agent is a SKILL.md with a declared role, declared inputs, a pipeline and
non-negotiable rules. When our reviewer rejected our own output, the fix was a
contract change (add R011, require a dated URL), not a luckier prompt - so the
fix survived the next run because it was written down. Second: multi-agent
orchestration without a framework. The Orchestrator skill is the state machine,
so every hand-off is a readable file and a judge can trace the run by opening
four Markdown documents. Third: the file system as state, which gave us
reproducibility with no database and no tracing UI.

Key insight, and the most transferable one: grounding has two halves and most
teams only build the first. Attribution asks whether a sentence carries a
citation; resolution asks whether that citation points at a source that actually
says this. Our first version only had attribution, and it passed its own review
while being wrong. A tag is not a citation.

Second insight: a rulebook is a product artefact, not documentation. We found our
audit log citing rules R009-R011 that did not exist anywhere - the agent was
enforcing rules it had invented. Writing them down formally was the difference
between a guardrail and a vibe.

Third: the reviewer must not be able to fix. Separating veto from rewrite keeps
the reviewer honest.

Where WorkBuddy did not help, stated plainly: cross-document consistency across
five formats was still manual. The agent would fix a fact in the lesson plan and
leave the same fact stale in the slide outline. We caught it with a text search,
not with agent reasoning. That needs its own gate and we do not have one yet.
